How Many Penalties Does Each Team Take in a Shootout?

In a standard 11-a-side football match, each team is normally given five penalty kicks at the beginning of a shootout. These kicks are taken alternately, meaning one team takes a kick before the other team takes its next attempt. The aim is to score more penalties than the opposition.

However, five is not always the final number of kicks. A shootout can finish before both teams complete their first five attempts if the score makes it mathematically impossible for one side to catch up. If both teams have taken five kicks and remain level, the contest moves into additional rounds.

Remember: Five kicks per team is the standard opening format, not a guaranteed total. A shootout may involve fewer kicks or continue beyond the first ten attempts.

How Does a Football Penalty Shootout Work?

A penalty shootout is used when a competition needs to produce a winner after a match finishes level and its regulations require a decisive result. Depending on the competition, penalties may follow normal time or extra time.

Once the match has ended, the referee organises the shootout. The teams take turns kicking from the penalty mark, with one player attempting to score against the opposing goalkeeper. The referee records each attempt and makes sure the procedure follows the Laws of the Game.

The standard opening sequence follows a simple pattern:

First kick Team A takes the opening penalty.
Second kick Team B takes its first penalty.
Third and fourth kicks Both teams take their second attempts.
Fifth to tenth kicks The teams continue alternating until each has taken five, unless the result is already decided.

This alternating arrangement gives both sides the same number of opportunities during the opening round. It also creates pressure because a missed attempt can change the situation for the next player.

Can a Penalty Shootout End Before Five Kicks?

Yes. The five-kick format does not mean every player must complete an attempt. The shootout ends as soon as one team has scored more goals than the other team could possibly achieve with its remaining kicks.

For example, imagine Team A has scored four goals from its first four attempts, while Team B has scored only one goal from its first four. Team B has just one opening-round kick remaining. Even if it scores, its total would be two, which is fewer than Team A's four. The shootout is therefore over.

Team A 4 4
Team B 1 4

Team B cannot catch Team A with only one kick left. Team A wins without needing to take its fifth penalty.

This rule prevents unnecessary attempts once the winner is already known. It also means that a team can win a shootout without using all five of its initial penalties.

What Happens If Both Teams Score the Same Number of Penalties?

If both teams have completed five kicks and the score remains level, the shootout moves into sudden death. This is the stage where every additional attempt becomes especially important.

In sudden death, teams continue taking one kick each in alternating order. The winner is decided when one team scores and the other team fails to score from the same number of attempts. Both sides must have taken an equal number of kicks for the result to count.

A simple sudden-death example

After five 4 goals 4 goals Level
Round 6 Scores Misses Team A wins

In this example, Team A wins because both sides have taken six kicks and Team A has scored one more goal. If both players scored, the shootout would continue to another round.

There is no fixed maximum number of kicks in the standard sudden-death procedure. The contest can continue through multiple rounds until a winner is determined.

Who Takes the Penalties in a Shootout?

Teams choose their penalty takers from the players who are eligible under the competition's rules and the Laws of the Game. In a standard senior match, this generally means players who were on the field or temporarily off it for a permitted reason when the match ended.

Coaches usually select players based on confidence, technique, experience and their ability to handle pressure. A team may prefer experienced forwards or midfielders, but there is no requirement that only attacking players take penalties. Defenders and goalkeepers can also be selected if eligible.

Can the same player take two penalties?

Not during the initial rotation while other eligible teammates have not yet taken their first kicks. Each kick must be taken by a different player until all eligible players on that team have had an opportunity.

If the shootout continues after everyone eligible has taken a kick, players can take another attempt. Teams may also change the order of their kickers in later rounds.

Important Rules Every Fan Should Know

The five-kick format is only one part of a penalty shootout. Several additional rules help keep the process fair and consistent for both teams.

  • Alternating attempts: Teams take kicks one after the other rather than completing all their attempts at once.
  • Different players: Each eligible player must take a first kick before a teammate can take a second one.
  • Goalkeeper participation: Goalkeepers defend penalties and may also take a kick if eligible.
  • Equal numbers: If one team has more eligible players than the other, the team with more players must reduce its numbers to match.
  • Referee records: The referee keeps track of the attempts and their outcomes.
  • Early conclusion: The shootout ends when one side has an unbeatable advantage.

These procedures are important because penalty shootouts are not simply informal contests between goalkeepers and penalty takers. They are governed by specific rules that determine who can participate and how the winner is decided.

Why Does the Order of Penalty Takers Matter?

Choosing the right players and deciding their order can influence how a team handles the pressure of a shootout. Although every penalty has the same basic objective, the psychological situation can change from one attempt to another.

Some coaches place confident penalty takers early because a successful start can settle the team. Others prefer to reserve certain players for later attempts, particularly when they expect the shootout to reach sudden death. There is no single order that guarantees success.

Confidence and pressure

A player may have excellent shooting technique but still struggle with the pressure of a decisive kick. The crowd, goalkeeper's movements and importance of the match can all affect concentration. Teams therefore consider both technical ability and mental readiness when selecting their takers.

Goalkeeper preparation

Goalkeepers also play an important role. They may study opponents' previous penalties, observe body movements and make decisions about which direction to dive. A save can change the momentum of the shootout, although the goalkeeper must still follow the relevant rules when facing each kick.

Common Misunderstandings About Penalty Shootouts

Every team must take exactly five penalties

This is incorrect. Five is the standard number of opening kicks, but a shootout can end earlier when the result becomes mathematically certain.

Sudden death begins after a single missed penalty

Not necessarily. A missed kick does not automatically start sudden death. The opening five-kick round continues unless one team gains an unbeatable lead. Sudden death begins when both teams have completed five kicks and remain level.

Penalty shootouts always follow extra time

Not in every competition. Tournament regulations determine whether a drawn match goes directly to penalties or includes extra time first. Fans should check the specific competition format rather than assume every match follows the same process.

A penalty shootout has a fixed maximum number of kicks

The standard procedure does not set a fixed maximum for sudden-death attempts. If the teams keep scoring at the same rate, the shootout continues until one side gains an advantage after an equal number of kicks.

How Penalty Shootouts Fit Into Football Competitions

Penalty shootouts are especially common in knockout football, where a competition must decide which team advances to the next round. They can appear in domestic cup matches, international tournaments and other competitions that require a winner.

League matches generally allow a draw, so they do not normally need a penalty shootout. Knockout competitions have different regulations, and some may use extra time before penalties. Understanding these formats makes it easier to follow the result of a match and know what happens next.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How many penalties does each team take first?

Each team normally takes up to five kicks in the initial round. The attempts are taken alternately, and the shootout may end earlier if one team has an unbeatable lead.

2. What happens if both teams score five penalties?

If both teams score the same number of goals after five attempts each, the shootout continues into sudden death. Each team takes another kick until one side leads after an equal number of attempts.

3. Can a team win after taking fewer than five penalties?

Yes. If the opposing team cannot mathematically catch up with its remaining kicks, the shootout ends immediately. The winning team does not need to take all five attempts.

4. Is there a limit to sudden-death penalties?

There is no fixed maximum in the standard procedure. Teams continue taking one kick each until one scores and the other fails to score from the same number of attempts.

5. Can a goalkeeper take a penalty in a shootout?

Yes. A goalkeeper can take a kick if eligible under the applicable rules. Goalkeepers are not restricted to defending the goal.

6. Can the same player take another penalty?

A player cannot take a second kick until all eligible teammates have taken their first kicks. After that, players can take further attempts if the shootout continues.

7. Do all football competitions use penalty shootouts?

No. Competition regulations decide how a tied match is resolved. Some competitions allow a draw, while others may use extra time, penalties or another permitted procedure.

8. Does a missed penalty mean the team loses?

No. One missed attempt does not automatically decide the result. The final outcome depends on the total goals scored and the number of kicks remaining.
